How it works

The pipeline

  1. Cleaning – removed nulls, negative fares and invalid passenger counts.
  2. Feature engineering – haversine distance (km) plus hour, day, month and weekday from the pickup time.
  3. Models – Linear Regression (R² ≈ 0.73) and Random Forest with 100 trees (R² ≈ 0.79).
  4. Serving – Flask loads the saved final_model.pkl and exposes a JSON API.

API

POST /api/predict with pickup_latitude, pickup_longitude, dropoff_latitude, dropoff_longitude, passenger_count, datetime.

POST /api/hourly · GET /api/stats · GET /api/history · GET /health

Good to know

  • Locations must be inside Hyderabad or Visakhapatnam, and both points in the same city. The underlying model was trained on NYC trips; Indian coordinates are mapped onto it and fares are calibrated to Indian cab rates (₹ base + per-km for each city), so treat results as estimates.
  • Distance is straight-line (haversine), the same as in training, not road distance.
  • The range shown is the 10th–90th percentile of the 100 individual trees' predictions.
  • Surge pricing, traffic, tolls and weather are not in the dataset, so the estimate excludes them.
